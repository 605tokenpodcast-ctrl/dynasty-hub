How draft grades work

What a pick produced. Rookie and startup picks: 60% the fantasy points the player scored in this league (for whoever rostered him, starters and bench) in his first 3 seasons, 40% his dynasty value today. Old classes whose players have mostly retired lean on points instead, since today's value says little about them. Redraft picks: his points that season. Both are measured against the average pick in the first 4 rounds of the same draft, so 1.00 is a solid early pick.

What the slot should produce. Each draft gets a smooth curve of how much each pick number returned in that class (early picks are expected to return far more). A pick is graded on what it produced minus what its slot should have, so a 1.01 bust hurts and a late-round hit shines.

Draft grade. Everything a manager's picks produced divided by what their slots should have (1.00× = as expected). Traded picks count for the manager who made the pick. Kickers, defenses and keepers are shown but not graded. The leaderboard combines every graded draft on a tighter scale, since luck evens out over many drafts, and ranks managers with at least 3 of them. Rookie classes younger than 3 seasons get an early grade that leans on today's values and firms up each season.

📜 All-Time Trade & Draft Pick Lineage Log

Complete history of trades with highlighted rookie draft selections. Dynasty trades are graded 3 years after the trade: 60% on the points each side's players scored for their new team in those 3 years, 40% on their dynasty value at the 3-year mark (picks count as the player drafted). Trades of only picks wait for the picks to be made and are graded after the rookies' first 3 seasons. Redraft trades are graded at the end of that season on points alone.
